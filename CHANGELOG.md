# Changelog

Written for Consumers: what a mod building against Fieldworks can use, or will see change. A published version never changes: a fix is the next patch (`docs/agents/releases.md`).

## Unreleased

## 0.4.0

- **Breaking:** patches and oil fields keep a clearance from anything of another kind ([ADR 0009](docs/adr/0009-patches-keep-a-clearance-and-the-rarer-start-wins.md), [#41](https://github.com/5thlayer/fieldworks/issues/41)). A start generates nothing when a start of another kind (a patch of another resource, or an oil field against a patch) would leave less than `clearance` between the two discs' edges; the rarer one, the kind whose structure set has the larger spacing, keeps its place, with oil fields first and then the resources in the order of `amounts.json` between equals. `clearance` is a new figure in the Patch numbers, `fieldworks/ore/amounts.json`, 16 blocks in the dimension's own blocks. Chunks generated after updating are laid out differently, so a seed's patches and oil fields change; those already in a world stay.

## 0.3.2

- A Harvester lights its power face, the Burner's firebox or the Electric's coil, and gives off light 13 while it works, and goes dark on any idle reason ([#42](https://github.com/5thlayer/fieldworks/issues/42)).

## 0.3.1

- Two adjacent drill heads no longer draw a wall between them. A casing block at a Harvester's bottom layer draws its own inner faces, which show only toward a drill head, and a drill head draws only the underside of the block above ([#39](https://github.com/5thlayer/fieldworks/issues/39)).

## 0.3.0

- A Harvester reaches down, never out ([ADR 0008](docs/adr/0008-a-harvester-reaches-down-never-out.md), [#34](https://github.com/5thlayer/fieldworks/issues/34)). Each of the nine columns under its footprint looks down from the block directly under it through air, replaceable blocks and fluids, up to the tier's new `reach` figure in `data/fieldworks/harvester_tier/` (0 when absent, no cap; `harvester_1` 1, `harvester_2` 2), and works the first other block it meets: an ore block, a harvestable block or an oil well. Reach is counted from the Harvester, so a column opened by a broken harvestable block looks further down but never past reach, and a spent ore block (stone) leaves it with no source.
- Drill heads show each column at work. Where a column's source lies directly under the Harvester its bottom block is drawn as a drill head; where it lies lower the Harvester places a `fieldworks:drill_head` block in the cell above it and draws drill legs up to its bottom block, and takes it away when the source changes or the Harvester goes. A drill head has no item, takes a replaceable block's or a fluid's place (waterlogging in a water source, destroying lava), collides with nothing, holds its cell, and breaking it dismantles the Harvester. Drill heads follow the world whether the Harvester is working or not. Every Harvester's bottom layer now draws itself block by block, with a new `drill_head` block state on the Harvester and its parts, and the drill head textures come from Malcolm Riley's collection.
- Patches for every mineral the -works Modules' recipes use in bulk ([ADR 0007](docs/adr/0007-a-patch-exists-for-every-mineral-a-module-recipe-uses.md), [#35](https://github.com/5thlayer/fieldworks/issues/35)). The set of ore resources grows from six to twelve: **redstone**, **gold** and **diamond** in the Overworld, and **quartz**, **glowstone** and **netherite** in the Nether, each with its ore block `fieldworks:<resource>_ore` (in `c:ores` and `c:ores/<resource>`), a Generation switch in `fieldworks-common.toml`, a map marker, and a structure set `fieldworks:patch_<resource>`. A draw pays what the vanilla block drops without Silk Touch: redstone dust, raw gold, a diamond, quartz, glowstone dust, and netherite scrap. The blocks need their vanilla counterparts' pickaxe tier; an exhausted Nether ore block becomes netherrack.
- Nether patches lie on the open Nether floor, found from under the bedrock roof, following the floor column by column within 8 blocks of the centre's and leaving lava out. `#fieldworks:patch_nether` (`#minecraft:is_nether`) is the biome tag they generate in. Nothing generates in the End.
- The Patch numbers, `fieldworks/ore/amounts.json`, gain `dimension` and `min_distance` for every resource: nothing generates within a resource's minimum distance of origin, in Overworld-equivalent blocks (a Nether centre counts eight times its distance). Sulfur 300, redstone 300, gold 500, uranium 600, diamond 1500, netherite 2000.
- Iron and coal swap their amount, density and radius factor: coal patches are richer and denser than iron ones.
- Magma slurry, `fieldworks:magma_slurry`, a Nether Drilling Fluid with a bucket, crafted from a lava bucket and soul soil. It is a real fluid, reachable through NeoForge's fluid capability, flowing at lava's pace but not in `#minecraft:lava`, no fuel, and it sets no crude into tar.
- Drilling Fluids belong to a dimension: `#fieldworks:drilling_fluids/overworld` holds drilling mud and `#fieldworks:drilling_fluids/nether` magma slurry; `#fieldworks:drilling_fluids` holds both.
- **Breaking:** uranium ore and oil wells need the Electric Harvester, and diamond and netherite ore too. Uranium and diamond also need drilling mud (10 mB a draw) and netherite magma slurry (25 mB), as written Harvest Recipes that replace the default, `fieldworks:uranium_from_ore`, `diamond_from_ore`, `netherite_scrap_from_ore` and `crude_from_oil_well`; a Burner Harvester can no longer work them, and an Electric Harvester works none until the recipe is held.
- **Breaking:** an oil well decayed to its floor pumps at the floor for good: nothing raises it. The drilling mud Harvest Recipe `fieldworks:crude_from_drilling_mud` is gone, and the Harvest Recipe codec no longer accepts `yield_condition` (ignored) or a crude result that `restores` (refused). A well's result can instead be `{"crude_oil": "cycle"}`, the well's own yield.
- **Breaking:** the new resources, and the minimum distances, apply only to chunks generated after updating: patches are structures, so an existing world keeps the land it has.
- A Harvester whose only sources need a written recipe held, as netherite, uranium, diamond and oil wells do, says so ("Needs its recipe held: pick it in EMI or JEI", `IdleReason.RECIPE_NEEDED`) in place of "Nothing under it to work".
- The Jade config entries for the Harvester and ore amount providers have names in lang, so a dev client with assertions on no longer crashes on the title screen with "Missing config translation".

## 0.2.1

- Both Harvesters are crafted at a crafting table. The Burner Harvester takes three iron ingots over cobblestone, a furnace and an iron pickaxe; the Electric Harvester takes a Burner Harvester ringed by iron ingots, copper ingots and redstone. Iron, copper, cobblestone and redstone are read from their `c:` tags, so another mod's may stand in. The recipes are `fieldworks:harvester_1` and `fieldworks:harvester_2`, which a datapack can override (#24).
- The iron, copper, coal, uranium and sulfur ore blocks are each in their `c:ores/<resource>` block tag, such as `c:ores/sulfur`; stone ore stays in plain `c:ores` only. Raw uranium is in `c:raw_materials/uranium` (#21).
- Fieldworks' items moved from the vanilla tabs (Natural Blocks, Ingredients, Tools & Utilities, Functional Blocks) to the suite's shared Works tab, libworks-runtime's `WorksTab.KEY`, and are in no other tab. Within it they run Harvesters by tier, the ore blocks, sulfur, raw uranium, the uranium ingot and tar, then the crude oil bucket and the drilling mud bottle and bucket (#4).
- Tar, `fieldworks:tar`, in `c:tar`: a source block of any fluid in `c:crude_oil` that touches lava, on any side, sets as a tar block, and the lava stays; flowing crude never sets. Breaking the tar block, or a piston pushing it, gives one tar, so a pipe outlet, a piston and a hopper can collect it. Nothing else makes tar: EMI shows it as a world interaction, and JEI as an info page on tar and on every crude source. Tar moves here from Wireworks, which takes `#c:tar` instead. Stand-in art (#22).
- The game tests' datapack, `gametest_pack/`, no longer ships in the jar, so `-Dfieldworks.gametestPack` no longer turns its recipes on in a Consumer's game; it was only ever for Fieldworks' own game tests (libworks ADR 0004).
- The Harvester's fuel, fill and drain slots veil their faded item in the suite's grey, the same as the other mods' machines: a touch darker and less opaque than before. The nested libworks-runtime is now 0.1.1 up to but not including 0.2 (#25).
- Sulfur ore blocks and the sulfur, raw uranium and uranium ingot items wear Malcolm Riley's unused textures (CC BY 4.0) in place of their generated stand-ins (#3).
- The nested libworks-runtime's range ends before 0.2, in place of 1.0: its next minor may break, as ADR 0001 sets. The jar also carries `NOTICE` and every licence text under `LICENSES/` (#17).

## 0.2.0

- Groundworks is now required, from 0.5.5 up to but not including 0.6, and is not nested: a player installs it beside Fieldworks. libworks-runtime 0.1.0 (mod id `libworks`) rides inside the jar by jar-in-jar (#5).
- The Burner Harvester, `fieldworks:harvester_1`, a 3×3×3 Groundworks footprint, draws the ore blocks in the layer under it one unit per operation, in turn, as a hand break does. It burns any fuel a hopper feeds it, a coal for eight operations as a furnace smelts eight, and pushes what it draws into the item handler at its front; with nowhere to put it, it stops and drops nothing. Its rank, speed and power come from `data/fieldworks/harvester_tier/harvester_1.json`, which a datapack can override; a missing or invalid file leaves it idle and the load logs why (#5).
- Harvest Recipes are data: a `fieldworks:harvesting` recipe in `data/<ns>/recipe/` names a `source` (a block, a block tag or a list of blocks), a `result` item, a `duration` in ticks at speed ×1, which a tier's `speed` divides, an optional `minimum_tier` (a Harvester block id, compared by rank) and `replaces_default`. With no Held recipe the Harvester runs only Default recipes, and skips an ore block whose Default recipe a recipe replaces; it works the rest of its footprint. A recipe the load refuses is logged with its id and why. A Held recipe, set as #10 describes, runs a written recipe in place of the Default recipes (#8).
- A Harvester over an oil well pumps it: one operation is one cycle of the well's yield and decay, into an 8,000 mB product tank, and the well never falls below its floor. While any well is under the footprint it works only the wells, in turn, and leaves the ore. A full tank stops it (#9).
- Crude oil has a bucket, `fieldworks:crude_oil_bucket`, a plain `BucketItem` with NeoForge's fluid item capability. Empty buckets a hopper feeds the Harvester come out of its output face filled with crude (#9).
- The Harvester exposes NeoForge's fluid capability on every face: a pipe can drain its crude and, from #11, fill only its Drilling Fluid tank. It also pushes crude into the fluid handler at its output face, and pulls nothing from it. The oil well itself still exposes no fluid capability (#9).
- The oil field marker on the FTB Chunks map draws the crude oil bucket in place of the vanilla bucket (#9).
- The Electric Harvester, `fieldworks:harvester_2`, ships at rank 2 and speed ×2 on FE alone: 40 FE per tick of work from a 50,000 FE buffer, taken through NeoForge's energy capability on every face of its footprint, which gives none back. A tier file's `power` may name `fuel`, `fe_per_tick` or both, with `fe_buffer`; a tier that takes both pays FE first and lights fuel only when the buffer is short of a tick's cost, and a fuel item already burning finishes before FE is spent again. A tier exposes only the power it declares: one that takes no FE has no energy capability, and one that takes no fuel accepts none. Default English names are "Burner Harvester" and "Electric Harvester", from lang (#7).
- A tier's `power.burn_per_operation` (default 200, as a furnace spends on a smelt) is the burn time one operation spends, however long the operation runs: one coal buys the Burner Harvester eight ore draws, or eight breaks of a harvestable block.
- The Harvester breaks every block in the `fieldworks:harvestable` block tag under its footprint, `#c:ores` by default, for its normal loot and leaves air: the loot table runs against an unenchanted pick and no tool is checked, so diamond ore breaks though a hand break would need an iron pick. Its Default recipe takes 40 ticks at speed ×1. Ore blocks are in `c:ores` too but are always drawn, never broken, whatever the tag holds. A footprint over ore blocks and harvestable blocks works them in turn, skips any other block, and, over an oil well, still pumps only the well. A Harvest Recipe's `source` may name a harvestable block, and its `result` replaces the block's loot. A pack sets what the Harvester breaks by replacing or extending the tag (#6).
- Harvest Recipes and the ore blocks' Default recipes show in EMI and JEI, in a `fieldworks:harvesting` tab with each recipe's source, result, duration at speed ×1 and minimum tier; a Default recipe a recipe replaces is not shown. Both viewers are optional. With a Harvester's menu open, a written recipe's `+` sets its Held recipe, which is saved with the block: the Harvester then works only the sources that recipe matches, and when it cannot run it idles and never falls back to the Default recipes. A Default recipe is not held. The server sends `fieldworks:harvesting` recipes to every client at login and on `/reload` (#10).
- Drilling mud, `fieldworks:drilling_mud`, is a fluid in the new `#fieldworks:drilling_fluids` tag, with `fieldworks:drilling_mud_bucket` (a plain `BucketItem`) and `fieldworks:drilling_mud_bottle`, a 250 mB container that turns into a glass bottle when emptied; both expose NeoForge's fluid item capability. A vanilla brewing stand brews the bottle from a water bottle with a clay ball or a slime ball. A Harvester has a Drilling Fluid tank of 8,000 mB: a filled container a hopper feeds it is emptied into the tank and the emptied one leaves by the output face with the results, one waiting in the Harvester while the tank has no room for all it holds, and a pipe can fill the tank with a fluid in the tag but never drain it, and the Harvester never pushes it out. A Harvest Recipe may name a `drilling_fluid`, `{ "fluid": <fluid, #fluid_tag or list>, "amount": <mB> }`; only the fluids in `#fieldworks:drilling_fluids` serve, and a recipe naming none of them loads with a warning and never runs. A Held recipe takes its mB from the tank when each operation completes, and idles with no fluid when the tank is short; with no Held recipe, nothing is ever taken. EMI and JEI show a recipe's fluid and mB beside its source (#11).
- A Harvester's menu shows its power (the burn left, in operations, and the FE buffered, only for the kinds its tier declares), the Drilling Fluid and product tanks, its output buffer, progress, the Held recipe and why it is idle. Its fuel, fill and drain slots are in the menu, the fuel slot only on a tier that takes fuel; an empty one shows, faded, each item it would take in turn, and EMI's and JEI's recipe and usage keys work on it. Jade's tooltip shows its state, its items as icons with their counts, the FE buffer and each tank as its own bar. A Clear button on the menu sends the server a packet that drops the Held recipe, returning the Harvester to its Default recipes; a recipe is still set only from EMI or JEI. The idle reason is the choice's own; a well whose product tank has no room for another cycle idles with "Output full", as a full output buffer does (#13).
- A well recipe can yield crude and be conditioned on the well's floor. A Harvest Recipe's `result` may be `{"crude_oil": <mB>}` for an oil well, the crude one operation pumps in place of the cycle's own yield, or `{"crude_oil": {"restores": <fraction>}}`, that fraction, above 0 and at most 1.0, of the yield the well had at its starting amount (either way the cycle still takes its step off the well, so it decays to its floor and no lower), and `yield_condition` is `above_floor` or `at_floor`: the recipe works a well only in that state, and with none it works a well either way. A Held recipe also takes its `drilling_fluid` when it pumps. Fieldworks ships `fieldworks:crude_from_drilling_mud`, `at_floor`: 25 mB of drilling mud restores a cycle to the well's starting yield, 10 mB for a well that started at 300,000 against its floor's 2 mB, while the Default recipe goes on pumping the floor rate with no fluid. One hopper carrying mud bottles and empty buckets together fills the drain and fill slots, and the glass bottles and crude buckets reach the output face. A recipe that names its source block by block is refused if its result or `yield_condition` does not suit an oil well. EMI and JEI list the oil well's Default recipe, with the crude oil bucket as the well's icon and crude per cycle falling to its floor, a restoring recipe with the share it restores and its crude at a normal well, and each `fieldworks:harvestable` block's Default recipe, a 40-tick break for its loot (#12).
- Superflat worlds generate ore patches and oil fields too, whatever structures their preset lists, where their biome is in `#fieldworks:patch_land` as anywhere else: the default preset's plains do, Water World's ocean does not. The structure sets they add are `#fieldworks:flat_worlds`.

## 0.1.0

- Ore patches of iron, copper, coal, stone, uranium and sulfur generate in the overworld's land beyond 150 blocks of the origin, each behind a switch in `fieldworks-common.toml`. Their ore blocks, `fieldworks:<resource>_ore`, are in `c:ores` and are mined by hand, one unit per break (#2).
- Oil fields place `fieldworks:oil_well` blocks whose crude decays to a floor and never runs out, behind a switch in `fieldworks-common.toml`. Crude oil, `fieldworks:crude_oil`, is in `c:crude_oil` (#2).
- `fieldworks:sulfur` is in `c:dusts/sulfur`, and `fieldworks:uranium_ingot`, smelted from `fieldworks:raw_uranium`, in `c:ingots/uranium` (#2).
- Every player's FTB Chunks map shows one marker per found patch, with what is left (#2).
- An ore block placed outside any patch starts at a default amount per resource, `default_amount` in `fieldworks/ore/amounts.json` (#2).
- Jade shows how much ore is left in an ore block (#2).
